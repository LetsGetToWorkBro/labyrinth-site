The honest version

WHAT IT IS

Most self-defense courses are a weekend of techniques performed on a partner who is cooperating. That is not how a real confrontation feels, and skills learned that way tend to evaporate the first time somebody actually grabs you. So we do not run one.

At Labyrinth, self-defense is taught inside the Brazilian jiu-jitsu programs themselves. BJJ is a grappling art: leverage, position and control instead of strikes. In class you practise escaping from underneath a heavier person, breaking grips, getting up safely and controlling somebody without having to hurt them, against training partners who are genuinely trying to stop you. That resistance is the whole point. A technique you have landed fifty times on a resisting partner is one you own; a technique you have only seen is one you have rented.

It runs six days a week in Fulshear for about a hundred and fifty children and a full room of adults, coached by black belts at an academy ranked in the top 1% nationally on jits.gg. The coaching that produces competition results is the same coaching a nervous beginner gets on their first Tuesday.

Who trains

WHO IT IS FOR

Three different worries walk through the same door.

Parents

The child being pushed around

A child who knows they can handle being grabbed or pinned stops carrying themselves like a target. Our kids classes (ages 3 to 15, in separated age groups) teach escapes, safe falling and control, with restraint built in: stop the situation, do not start a fight.

Women

The woman who wants a real answer

Free women's self-defense seminars through the year, including the Rolling for Ribbons seminar in October 2026, plus a place in every regular adult class. The skills are the unglamorous ones that matter: escaping pins, breaking grips, getting to your feet.

Adults

The adult who has never trained

Most adults who start here have never done a martial art. You do not need to be fit first. You learn against partners at your own pace, and the fitness arrives as a side effect of training twice a week.
